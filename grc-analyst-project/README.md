# GRC Analyst Project

## Overview

GRC assessment project developed for a fictional small-business environment, 24HR Dance Studio, to evaluate cybersecurity risk, governance documentation, third-party risk, incident response readiness, and alignment with recognized security frameworks.

The project follows a structured GRC assessment process from initial risk identification through control-gap analysis and remediation planning.

The environment is fictional and was created for portfolio and professional-development purposes. Publicly available information may have been used as reference material, but the findings and recommendations do not represent the actual cybersecurity posture of any real organization.

---

## Project Objectives

- Identify cybersecurity and operational risks
- Evaluate likelihood, impact, ownership, and residual risk
- Review security and privacy documentation
- Assess third-party vendor dependencies
- Evaluate incident response readiness
- Identify governance and control gaps
- Map findings to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework
- Review selected areas against ISO/IEC 27001:2022 concepts
- Develop prioritized remediation recommendations

---

# Project Deliverables

## 1. Cybersecurity Risk Assessment

Performed a cybersecurity risk assessment covering common risks associated with online booking, payments, customer data, employee access, social media, and third-party services.

The risk register evaluated **11 risk scenarios** using:

- Likelihood
- Business impact
- Recommended mitigation
- Risk ownership
- Residual risk
- Remediation priority

Key risks included:

- Weak authentication
- Shared employee accounts
- Customer data exposure
- Employee and customer phishing
- Missing backup procedures
- Third-party platform dependency
- Public Wi-Fi usage
- Online payment security
- Social-media impersonation

Priority recommendations included stronger authentication, MFA, role-based access, data backups, security awareness, and stronger customer-data protections.

[View Risk Assessment](./Simulation-Risk-Assessment-Report.pdf)

---

## 2. Security Policy Assessment

Reviewed publicly available documentation to identify gaps between identified cybersecurity risks and documented governance practices.

Areas evaluated included:

- Access control
- Data protection
- Privacy
- Security awareness
- Backup and recovery
- Social media
- Vendor management
- Incident response

The review identified documentation gaps involving MFA requirements, security-awareness training, backup and recovery procedures, vendor oversight, and incident response.

[View Policy Assessment](./24HR-Dance-Studio-Policy-Assessment.pdf)

---

## 3. Vendor Risk Assessment

Evaluated third-party services supporting business operations, customer communication, website availability, booking, and payments.

Vendors assessed included:

- Mindbody
- Squarespace
- Instagram

The assessment considered:

- Services provided
- Potential risks
- Business impact
- Risk rating
- Vendor dependency
- Recommended controls

Key recommendations included establishing vendor-management procedures, maintaining escalation contacts, conducting periodic vendor reviews, reviewing available security documentation, and maintaining contingency procedures.

[View Vendor Risk Assessment](./Simulated-Vendor-Risk-Assessment.pdf)

---

## 4. Incident Response Readiness Assessment

Evaluated the organization's preparedness to identify, respond to, and recover from cybersecurity incidents.

Scenarios assessed included:

- Social-media compromise and impersonation
- Employee phishing
- Customer-data exposure
- Third-party service outages

Readiness was evaluated across:

- Account security response
- Security awareness
- Data protection response
- Vendor incident management
- Business recovery

Priority recommendations included developing an incident response plan, defining response roles and responsibilities, establishing breach-notification procedures, and creating backup and recovery procedures.

[View Incident Response Readiness](./Incident-Response-Readiness.pdf)

---

## 5. NIST Cybersecurity Framework Review

Consolidated findings from the risk, policy, vendor, and incident-response assessments and mapped them to the six functions of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework:

- Govern
- Identify
- Protect
- Detect
- Respond
- Recover

The review identified opportunities to strengthen:

- Security governance documentation
- Vendor oversight
- Preventative controls
- Security-event reporting
- Incident response procedures
- Backup and recovery planning

Priority recommendations included:

- Develop an Incident Response Plan
- Implement MFA
- Establish vendor-management procedures
- Develop backup and recovery procedures
- Maintain periodic risk reviews
- Establish security-event reporting procedures
- Conduct periodic vendor reviews

[View NIST CSF Review](./24HR-Dance-Studio-Compliance-Review.pdf)

---

## 6. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Alignment Review

Performed a selected-control alignment review using findings from the existing risk, policy, vendor, and incident-response assessments.

Areas reviewed included:

- Information security policies
- Access control and authentication
- Security awareness
- Customer-data protection
- Third-party and supplier security
- Incident management
- Backup and recovery
- Business continuity
- Security monitoring and reporting

The review identified opportunities to strengthen documented controls and governance practices across several security areas.

Priority recommendations included:

- Establish formal security policies
- Implement MFA and stronger access controls
- Develop recurring security-awareness training
- Establish vendor-management procedures
- Develop an Incident Response Plan
- Document backup and recovery procedures
- Develop continuity procedures
- Establish security monitoring and reporting processes

This review is a portfolio-based alignment assessment and does not represent an ISO/IEC 27001 certification audit or formal determination of conformity.

[View ISO 27001 Alignment Review](./ISO-27001-Alignment-Review.pdf)

# Key Findings

Across the project, recurring security and governance gaps included:

- Weak authentication and access-control practices
- Limited security governance documentation
- Missing incident-response procedures
- Third-party oversight gaps
- Missing backup and recovery procedures
- Security-awareness gaps
- Vendor and service dependencies
- Limited security-event reporting and monitoring

# Assessment Methodology

The project followed a progressive GRC assessment process:

1. Identify cybersecurity and operational risks
2. Evaluate likelihood and business impact
3. Assign risk ownership and remediation priority
4. Identify residual risk after recommended controls
5. Review available policies and security documentation
6. Assess third-party dependencies and vendor risks
7. Evaluate incident-response readiness
8. Map findings to NIST CSF
9. Review selected findings against ISO/IEC 27001 concepts
10. Develop prioritized remediation recommendations
